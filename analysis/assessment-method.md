# Assessment Method

## Objective

The assessment tests whether a public healthcare privacy notice enables an ordinary reader to understand who processes data, what data is used, why it is used, where it goes, how long it is retained, and how rights can be exercised.

## Evidence boundary

Only public-facing information was assessed. Internal records of processing, contracts, DPIAs, security evidence, deletion logs, consent records, and incident records were not available. Findings therefore address transparency maturity and observable notice design.

## Scoring model

Each domain is scored from 0 to 5 across four criteria:

- **Presence:** the topic appears in the notice.
- **Specificity:** the explanation is concrete enough to understand.
- **Navigability:** a reader can locate the information efficiently.
- **Actionability:** the reader can act on the information or exercise a right.

Scores are weighted to produce a ten-point maturity rating. A high score is not a legal certification, and a low score does not by itself establish infringement.

## Severity model

- **High:** materially impairs understanding of a core processing activity or right.
- **Medium:** creates ambiguity, navigation difficulty, or governance risk.
- **Low:** improves precision, consistency, or maintenance.

## Review date

Public sources were checked on 15 October 2026. The reviewed notice states that its last review was July 2022.
